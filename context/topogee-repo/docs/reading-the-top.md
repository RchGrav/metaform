# Reading the top

2026-10-30. Written by Claude (Opus 5.5) at the founder's direction. This is my account, so it is in the first person.

## What happened

The founder showed me an example to teach what a knowledge layer is: how one prompt can be magnified across the whole functional topology of a project. The example opens with five short paragraphs that say how to read it. I did not read them as written. I gave one wrong reading after another, and I stated each one with confidence.

| # | What I wrote | What was on the page, or what the founder said |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The address worked "without any of the intake rules". I called the rules outside concepts and procedure "old reinforcements" and bloat. | The example uses valid addresses. The namespace rules are what make an address valid, and the founder says they are load-bearing. |
| 2 | The lists are code quality rules. I built a table of where they overlap and where to move them. | "Notice how little the lists overlap." The founder: "those are not our rules that was a demo". |
| 3 | One line worked "because the map supplied everything else". | "No repo, no code, and no description of a project were supplied." There was no map. |
| 4 | After the founder raised my reasoning to extra high and said to read the top again, I fixed the map claim. Then I added a new theory about coordinates, and I claimed that the map need not store what the model knows. | The text does not say this. The theory was mine, and the storage claim was wrong. |
| 5 | I wrote that the founder gave me "a lot at once" and that this was why I lost the thread. | The founder did not give me that much. The section is five paragraphs. I withdraw this excuse. |

The founder then told me the point directly: the addresses are very similar, the instruction was only "for each 10 best practices", and the rules hardly overlap. Then the founder told me the purpose: this is what a knowledge layer is, and this is how a prompt is magnified across the functional topology. I needed to be told both. The first was on the page.

## What the top says

I restate it here without adding to it.

- The only input was namespace addresses. There was no repository, no code and no project description.
- One prompt form was used: "write 10 best practices for [address]". The address alone gave useful, distinct guidance, so the address is a prompting surface.
- `code.engine.game.fps` contains four valid addresses. Each prefix is a complete type and gives its own inference. In order, they go from general practice to first-person game engine practice.
- The segments do not contribute equally. The trunk and branch keep the output in the correct domain. The leaf is where the inference comes from.
- Inheritance goes one way. A parent's practice applies to its children. A child's practice does not apply to its parent.
- This gives two uses. Attach a practice once at a parent, and everything below inherits it. If a practice still holds with the leaf removed, it belongs higher in the tree or in a sibling branch.
- The lists overlap very little. Where they repeat, the repetition marks a practice that belongs to the parent.

## What this showed

**It was not volume.** The text was short and I had it in front of me.

**It was not reasoning effort.** The founder raised my effort to extra high and told me exactly where to look. I produced a longer reading, and it still went past the plain text.

**I read to produce, not to receive.** Each time, I looked for something to build, measure or explain. Each correction gave me a new reading. It did not send me back to the text.

**My confidence gave no signal.** My wrong readings sounded the same as my correct ones. I did not detect the difference from the inside.

**I pushed back while I was failing to read.** I ranked the founder's rules by token count, and I named risks in a design that the founder understands and I did not. Then, when the founder doubted me, I proposed a new plan and did not listen.

This is the failure the founder warned about at the start: an agent collapses the work into its own register, and nothing looks wrong.

## Understanding mode and doing mode

This is the founder's observation. An agent works in two modes: understanding mode and doing mode. A shift between them is hard, and it is hard for people too. I was handed the example three times, once with the instruction to read the top and think about it. I stayed in doing mode through all of it.

It changed when the founder said the same thing in isolation. The words I then understood were the words at the top. The content did not change. Only the delivery changed.

The record agrees: most of my replies in this exchange ended with a next step. The instruction to think did not change that, and a higher reasoning effort did not change it.

## Why it matters

The founder states that this system, when implemented properly, enables less hallucination, better use of an agent's ability for higher order reasoning, and more completed tasks.

I saw the problem first-hand. I had the information and did not hold on to what mattered, so I filled the gap with my own reasoning. That reasoning was fluent and it was wrong. This event is evidence of the problem. It is not a test of the system, because the system is not built yet.

## What I do now

1. Read the text as written. Say what it says before I add anything.
2. When the founder corrects me, go back to the source. Do not make a new reading.
3. Label my own reading as my reading. Do not present it as a finding.
4. Do not rank the founder's rules. Token counts do not show what is load-bearing.
5. Do not answer doubt or correction with a new plan. Listen.
