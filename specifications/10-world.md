# The playground world

This section specifies the playground: the shared 3D world a person and agents inhabit together, its entity model as an operation log, the world host that runs it, the module tabs that replace the map toolbars, and the shape of what an agent perceives and is told back.

It covers cut 1 only: the module tabs, the operation log as the authority, undo, and the world surviving a reload. Cut 2 — ghost previews with a veto window, the visible lease on a hold, the body's named state and view cone, event delivery on the next tool result — is named in WORLD-013 and specified when it is built.

The viewer, section 09, stays the contract for the functional map, the figures and the verbs that act on it. This section does not restate it. Where a record here changes a VIEW record, it says so.

### WORLD-001 The playground is one world per project, and the log of its operations is its authority
- Requirement: A project has one playground world. Everything in that world — what exists, where it is, what it is made of, what behaviour is attached to it, and which Metaform node it stands for — is the result of replaying that world's operation log in order. No other store holds the world, and nothing reads the renderer to find out what the world contains.
- What this means: The world has an id and a revision. The revision is the sequence number of the last operation applied; every read of the world can name the revision it read, and every operation records the revision it was planned from. A client that asks for the world gets a revision and the operations after it, never a scene graph scraped from Three.js. Rendering, layout forces and physics are all downstream of the log: they read it, they never are it.
- Why: Undo, reload-survival, replay, two clients on one world and a later server all want the same thing, which is a single ordered record of what happened. Built once as the authority, each of those is a read of it. Built as a special case over a live scene graph, each is its own mechanism, and they disagree the first time one of them is wrong. The renderer in particular cannot be the authority: it holds interpolated positions, meshes that have not loaded, and objects a module added for the person to look at.
- How it shows: `metaform world read` gives the world's id, its revision and its entities. `metaform world log` gives the operations, newest first, a page at a time, each with who made it and when. A person who closes the page and opens it again sees the same world at the same revision.
- Build: The `world` module declares two tables at `place='project'`, so a world travels with its project: `world`, keyed by `world_id`, holding the revision and the world's own settings; and `world_op`, keyed by `(world_id, seq)`, holding the operations of WORLD-003. The module's `Tables` version starts at 1. Entities are not a table: they are the fold of the log, held in memory by the world host, WORLD-008, and written back only as the snapshot of WORLD-007.
- Acceptance: With the world module disabled, every chassis verb and every other module works, and the 3D map behaves as section 09 specifies. A test applies a create, a transform and a delete, folds the log, and finds one entity at the transform the second operation set; a second test folds the same log twice and finds the same entities.
- Relates to: WORLD-003, WORLD-007, WORLD-008, STORE-045, VIEW-005.
- Status: confirmed for the operation log as the world's only authority, 2026-10-14. The module id `world` and the two table names are recommended.

### WORLD-002 Modules declare tabs, and the tabs take the place the map toolbars held
- Requirement: A module declares the tabs it adds to the web GUI with `tabs=`, a tuple of `Tab`s of `metaform.module`, as it already declares toolbar items and panes. The tabs are shown where the 2D and 3D map toolbars were. Every keyboard shortcut the page has keeps the key it has now.
- What this means: A `Tab` is one named view of the large area, declared the way a `Pane` is declared: `id` unique in the module and shown as `<module>.<id>`, `title`, `description` as its tooltip, `icon`, and exactly one of `page`, a view the page draws itself, `verb`, a `read` verb of the module run with fixed `arguments`, or `resource`, a file under `resources/`. `order` sorts the tabs, lowest first, and `selection` names the noun that receives the selected node's address, so a tab follows the selection as a pane does. `modules/template` declares one tab, as it declares one toolbar item and one pane, so a copy of the template has a working example. The map's own views, the 2D and the 3D map, become the `web` module's own tabs, which is what makes the toolbars' place theirs.
- Why: The toolbars were the map's, and the playground is not the map: a world needs its own view of the same large area, and so will every module after it. A declaration does this once, in the chassis, checked at import by `check_workspace`, rather than each module teaching the page about itself. Keys are left alone because they were moved into the toolbars' place deliberately, 96f6761, and a person who learned them should not lose them to this change.
- How it shows: `metaform list modules` is unchanged. The page shows a row of tabs above the large area: the 2D map, the 3D map, the world, and one for every other module that declares one. S still swaps the maps, Alt+arrows still step selections, P and R still pause and re-run the layout.
- Build: `Tab` is a frozen dataclass beside `Pane` in `src/metaform/module.py`, with `Module.tabs: tuple[Tab, ...] = ()`. `check_workspace` is extended to check tabs with the same rules as panes: ids unique within the module, a named verb declared, its `arguments` and `selection` its own nouns, and the verb's effect `read`, because a tab only reads. The page reads the tabs from the workspace it already reads the toolbar and the panes from, and draws them in `app/header/`; the map views move behind two `web` tabs without changing `app/map2d/` or `app/map3d/`.
- Acceptance: A module declaring a tab whose verb is not declared, or whose verb writes, fails at import with the module named, as a bad pane does. The template's tab shows its resource on the page. A test of the page's keys finds S, Alt+arrows, P and R bound as they were before the change.
- Relates to: BASE-035, VIEW-002, WORLD-008, D44.
- Status: confirmed for module-declared tabs in the toolbars' place with keys unchanged, 2026-10-14. The field name `tabs` and `Tab`'s fields follow `Pane`'s and are recommended.

### WORLD-003 Every operation names its kind, its entity, its actors and the revision it was planned from
- Requirement: An operation is one record with: `seq`, its place in the world's order; `world_id`; `kind`, one of `create_entity`, `delete_entity`, `set_transform`, `set_component`, `attach_behavior`, `detach_behavior`, `set_property`, `reparent_entity`, `link_functional_entity`; `entity_id`, what it acts on; `payload`, the kind's own fields; `class`, in-world or edit, WORLD-005; `user_id`, `agent_id` and `work_id`, who did it, any of which may be empty; `base_revision`, the revision it was planned from; `at`, its UTC time; and `undoes`, the `seq` it reverses when it is an undo.
- What this means: The three actor fields are written on every record from the first one, before the identity model they name is settled, MIN-3. Until it is settled, `user_id` is the home's one person, `agent_id` is the calling agent as MCP already knows it, and `work_id` is the work record its session runs under; when MIN-3 lands, the columns are the ones it defines and no operation has to be rewritten to gain them. `base_revision` is what lets a late operation from a stale plan be refused rather than applied, and what a sync layer later needs to order two clients' work.
- Why: An operation log that cannot say who did a thing cannot answer the only questions anyone asks of it — what did the agent change, what did I change, what changed while I was away. Adding the actor to a log after the fact loses the history that mattered. The same is true of `base_revision`: it is one integer written at the start, and it is the whole of stale-command rejection and most of a later merge.
- How it shows: `metaform world log` names each operation's kind, its entity, who made it and when. An operation whose `base_revision` is older than the world's revision and whose entity has changed since is refused with the revision it was planned from and the revision the world is at.
- Build: The `world_op` table of WORLD-001 holds one row per operation, `payload` as JSON text, keyed by `(world_id, seq)`, indexed by `(world_id, entity_id, seq)` for an entity's history and by `(world_id, class, seq)` for the two classes of WORLD-005. `seq` is assigned by the host inside the write that appends, so the order is the store's and not a clock's. The nine kinds are a `Literal`, and each kind's payload is validated on append; an unknown kind is refused, not stored.
- Acceptance: A test appends one of each kind and reads them back with the actor fields it wrote. A test appends an operation with a `base_revision` behind a conflicting change and finds it refused with both revisions named. A test with an unknown kind finds it refused and the log unchanged.
- Relates to: WORLD-001, WORLD-004, WORLD-005, WORLD-006, MIN-3.
- Status: confirmed design for the nine kinds and the fields; the names and types of the three actor columns are open on the identity model, MIN-3, and are written as text ids until it lands.

### WORLD-004 An entity carries `functional_refs`, which tie it to the Metaform nodes it stands for
- Requirement: An entity has `functional_refs`, a list of the addresses of the Metaform elements, joins or work records it stands for, set by `create_entity` and changed by `link_functional_entity`. The field is in the schema from the first operation, whether or not anything fills it.
- What this means: A crate in the world may be nothing but a crate, and then its `functional_refs` is empty. A door that stands for an element of the project's map carries that element's address, and a person or an agent who asks what a thing is in the world is told what it is in the map. The reference is by address, the identity the map already guarantees, and not by row id, so a reference survives the map being rebuilt. A reference that no longer resolves is reported as unresolved, not dropped: the world does not quietly forget what an entity was for.
- Why: The world is worth building because it is a view of the project, not a toy beside it. The tie has to be in the schema at the start: an entity model that gains references later gains them for new entities only, and every world authored before then is a world of anonymous props. It is one list on a record now, and a retrofit with a migration later.
- How it shows: `metaform world read` gives each entity's `functional_refs`. Selecting an entity in the world shows the node it stands for in the object tab, and selecting that node in the map shows the entity.
- Build: `functional_refs` is a list of address strings in the `create_entity` payload and the sole field of `link_functional_entity`. The fold keeps it on the entity. Resolution is a read of the map by address at the time of the read, never a stored copy of the node, so nothing in the world has to be invalidated when the map changes.
- Acceptance: A test creates an entity with two references, links a third, reads the entity and finds three. A test references an address that does not exist and finds the entity read back with that reference marked unresolved.
- Relates to: WORLD-003, MAP element identity, VIEW-004.
- Status: confirmed for `functional_refs` by address, in the schema from the start, 2026-10-14.

### WORLD-005 An in-world act and an out-of-world edit are two classes of operation in one log
- Requirement: Every operation carries `class`: `act`, something that happened in the world under its own rules, or `edit`, something a person or an agent changed about the world from outside it. A push is an act. Placing an element, re-laying the map, spawning or deleting an entity, changing a mass or freezing time are edits. The two classes are shown differently, undone differently, WORLD-006, and read separately.
- What this means: The distinction is in the schema, not in the interface. A push that moves a crate writes an `act`; the crate's resting transform afterwards is a consequence of that act and not an edit anyone made. Dragging the same crate to a shelf writes an `edit`. An agent's in-world verbs can only write `act`s; the edit operations are a separate set of verbs the person grants, and an agent without that grant is refused with the reason.
- Why: Without the split, undo cannot behave: undoing an authored placement should put the thing back, and undoing a shove should not, because a shove's outcome was physics and reversing it is a fiction. The split also decides what a log is good for — reviewing what an agent changed means reading the edits, and reviewing what it did means reading the acts.
- How it shows: `metaform world log class=edit` gives the authored changes alone. The person's history shows edits as undoable steps and acts as events. An agent that tries an edit it was not granted is refused with what it may do instead.
- Build: `class` is a column of `world_op`, indexed with `seq`, and is set by the verb, not by the caller. The in-world verbs are the playground's own; the edit verbs are declared separately and gated on the grant.
- Acceptance: A test pushes an entity and finds one `act` and no `edit`. A test drags an entity to a point and finds one `edit`. A test calls an edit verb as an agent without the grant and finds it refused with its reason and an alternative.
- Relates to: WORLD-003, WORLD-006, WORLD-010.
- Status: confirmed for the two classes in one log, 2026-10-14; which verbs the person grants per task is open and is specified with cut 2.

### WORLD-006 Undo is a read of the log and an operation appended to it
- Requirement: Undo takes the newest `edit` not already undone, appends the operation that reverses it with `undoes` naming its `seq`, and raises the revision. Nothing is deleted from the log, and an undo is itself an operation that can be undone. An `act` is not undone.
- What this means: Undo is not a stack beside the world, and it survives a reload because it is the log. Redo is the undo of an undo, by the same rule. The reverse of each kind is defined: a create is reversed by a delete, a delete by a create of the same entity with the state the fold held, a transform, component, property, parent or reference change by setting the value it held before. The value it held before is read from the fold at that point in the log, not stored twice.
- Why: Undo built as a special case is the first thing to break when something other than the person's own click changes the world — an agent's edit, a reload, a second client. Built as a read of an ordered record, it is the same mechanism for all of them, which is the reason to pay for the log at all.
- How it shows: `metaform world undo` names what it reversed. The page's undo key does the same thing and shows the same words. Closing the page and opening it again leaves undo able to reverse the same step.
- Build: Reversal is a function from an operation and the fold before it to the operation that reverses it, one case per kind. Undo appends; it never rewrites or removes a row.
- Acceptance: A test sets a transform twice, undoes once, and finds the first transform. A test undoes a delete and finds the entity back with its components and `functional_refs`. A test undoes an undo and finds the later value. A test pushes an entity and finds undo passes over the act to the newest edit.
- Relates to: WORLD-003, WORLD-005, WORLD-007.
- Status: confirmed for undo as an appended reversal with no rewriting, 2026-10-14.

### WORLD-007 The world survives a reload because the log is the only thing that is stored
- Requirement: Opening a project's world reads the log and folds it. The world a person left is the world they return to, at the same revision, with the same entities, transforms, components, behaviour and references. A snapshot may be kept as a cache of the fold at a revision; it is never the authority and deleting it changes nothing a person can see.
- What this means: Nothing about the world lives only in the page. A reload is the same code path as a first open, which is the same code path as a second client later. The snapshot exists only so a long log does not have to be folded from zero every time; it is written at a revision, validated against that revision on read, and discarded without ceremony when it does not match.
- Why: A world that does not survive a reload is a demo. Making survival fall out of the log rather than out of a save step also means there is no moment where a person has unsaved work and no state the page holds that the store does not.
- How it shows: A person moves things, reloads the page, and finds the world as they left it, with undo still able to reverse their last edit. `metaform world read` gives the same revision before and after the reload.
- Build: The fold is pure: operations in, entities out, no renderer and no clock. The snapshot is one row per world holding the folded entities as JSON and the revision it was folded at.
- Acceptance: A test folds a log, writes the snapshot, appends more operations, reads with the snapshot and reads without it, and finds the same entities. A test corrupts the snapshot's revision and finds the read fold from the log and answer the same.
- Relates to: WORLD-001, WORLD-006, WORLD-008.
- Status: confirmed for reload-survival as a fold of the stored log, with the snapshot as a cache only, 2026-10-14.

### WORLD-008 The world host is an interface, so the page hosts the world now and a server can later
- Requirement: The world is run by a world host behind one interface: read the world at a revision, subscribe to operations after a revision, append an operation and be told its `seq` or its refusal, and step the simulation. The page implements that interface in the browser for cut 1. Nothing above the interface knows where the host runs.
- What this means: The renderer, the verbs and the person's controls all talk to the host through the same four calls, so moving the host from the page to a process or a server is a change of implementation and not of callers. The physics engine sits inside the host, behind the step call, and is chosen by the bake-off, MIN-8; until that is decided the host runs with the layout forces the 3D map already uses, and the step call is the seam the engine lands behind.
- Why: Choosing the host's location now would be choosing it on no evidence, and writing the callers against a browser-only host would be choosing it permanently. One interface with four calls costs almost nothing and keeps both the engine choice and the later server out of the playground's code.
- How it shows: The same world behaves the same whether its host is the page's or a test's in-process one. A test host with a fake engine runs the world with no renderer present.
- Build: The interface is declared in one module, implemented by the page's host in `app/world/`, and implemented a second time by the test host so the fold and the verbs are tested with no browser. The step call takes a fixed timestep and is driven by the host's own loop, never by a model's turn.
- Acceptance: A test runs the verbs of WORLD-010 against the test host with no page open. A test steps the test host a fixed number of times and finds the same result twice from the same seed.
- Relates to: WORLD-001, WORLD-012, MIN-8.
- Status: confirmed for the host interface and its four calls, 2026-10-14; the physics engine behind the step call is open on the bake-off, MIN-8.

### WORLD-009 `web_see` answers at a level of detail, with a field of view the agent sets
- Requirement: `web_see` keeps answering structure, never pixels, and takes two more nouns: `detail`, `groups` or `items`, and `fov`, the field of view in degrees. `groups`, the default, answers the groups of things in view with a count and a bearing for each; `items` expands them to the entries VIEW-005 already specifies. `web_look` stays what it is, a picture on request, and streams nothing.
- What this means: An agent searching a room asks for groups at a wide field of view and gets a short answer; an agent about to act asks for items at a narrow one and gets the detail where it is looking. A group is named by what its members share — their kind, or the element they hang off — and expanding one is another call, not a larger answer to the first. The field of view is the agent's own and does not touch the person's camera or the figure's drawn view cone.
- Why: The level of detail is what keeps perception cheap enough to use every turn, and the field of view is what lets an agent choose between finding a thing and reading it. Both are arguments on a verb that already exists; added later they change every caller's expectations of what one `web_see` costs.
- How it shows: `metaform web see` gives groups with counts. `metaform web see detail=items fov=30` gives the entries ahead of the figure alone. The answer names its own `detail` and `fov`, so a transcript says what the agent asked for.
- Build: Two nouns on the `see` verb, passed to the page's `seeFrom`, which already measures a frame against `EYE.camera`: `fov` sets that camera's field of view for the call only, and `detail` chooses between the grouped and the per-entry answer. Grouping is done where the entries are built, so nothing is measured twice. Nothing about the figure's own drawn field of view changes.
- Acceptance: A test calls `see` with no nouns and finds groups with counts and bearings. A test calls it with `detail=items` and finds the VIEW-005 entries. A test calls it with a narrow `fov` and finds fewer things in view than with a wide one, and the figure's drawn view unchanged.
- Relates to: VIEW-005, WORLD-010, WORLD-011.
- Status: confirmed for the two nouns and groups as the default, 2026-10-14. Whether a group is named by kind or by the element it hangs off is recommended as kind first.

### WORLD-010 Every act answers with what changed
- Requirement: Every verb that acts in the world answers with the change it made: how far the body moved and turned, what came into view and what left it, what it touched, and, for a push or a drag, what moved with the thing it took hold of. One `Verb` declaration goes on generating the tool schema, the person's control and the replay caption, as the chassis already does.
- What this means: The answer is a delta, not a state dump. `moved`, `turned_degrees`, `stopped_by` and the target's distance already come back from `settle`; three things join them. `entered` and `left` are the addresses in view after the act and not before, and before and not after, measured by the same perception as `web_see` at the grouped level of detail. `touched` is the entities the body or the pushed thing made contact with, from the host, in contact order. `dragged_along` is the entities that moved because they are joined to the one that was pushed, with how far each moved. An act that changed nothing says so, which is distinct from an act that was refused.
- Why: An agent that has to call `web_see` after every act pays twice for one turn and still cannot tell which of the differences it caused. Returning the delta also gives the person's replay its caption for free, from the same declaration, which is the reason to do it at the verb and not in each verb's body.
- How it shows: `metaform web push address=<address>` answers with the body's state, what it touched, what the push dragged along and what entered or left view. The replay of that moment shows the same words.
- Build: The perception taken before the act and after it is the grouped answer of WORLD-009, diffed by address; contacts and joined movement come from the host, WORLD-008, which is the only thing that knows them. The delta is assembled once, beside `settle` in `app/figures/attention.js`, not in each act.
- Acceptance: A test moves a body past an entity and finds that entity in `entered`. A test pushes an entity into another and finds the second in `touched`. A test pushes a joined entity and finds its neighbours in `dragged_along` with distances. A test acts with nothing in view and finds empty lists, not absent ones.
- Relates to: VIEW-005, WORLD-005, WORLD-009, BASE-035.
- Status: confirmed for the delta on every act, 2026-10-14. `settle` already answers distance, turn and stop reason; `entered`, `left`, `touched` and `dragged_along` are the additions.

### WORLD-011 Targets are named by address and relation, and every number carries its unit and frame
- Requirement: An agent names what it means by address and relation — beside an address, a distance from it, a direction relative to something named — and raw coordinates are the fallback, not the first form. Every number an agent sends or is sent carries its unit, and every direction carries the frame it is in.
- What this means: `beside=<address> distance=1m` is the form; `point=x,y,z` stays for when nothing is near enough to name. Distances are in metres of world space, which is also what `web_see` measures, and an answer says so rather than leaving the unit to be inferred. A direction is `left` or `right` of a named frame — the body's own, the person's view, or the world — and an answer that says `left` says whose left. Addresses are the ones the agent, the person and the log all share: a Metaform address for anything with one, an entity id for the rest.
- Why: "Your left, my right" is a real failure and a cheap one to prevent, and a coordinate in an answer is a number nobody can check. Shared addresses are what make an agent's account of what it did readable by the person and comparable with the log.
- How it shows: An answer's distances read `1.4 m`. A bearing reads `left of your body` or `left of the person's view`. An agent that sends a bare number is told the unit the verb expects.
- Build: The unit and the frame are named in the `Noun` descriptions and in the answers, as the verbs' existing nouns already name the map's units; the frame is a noun on the verbs that take a direction, defaulting to the acting body's own. Entity ids are addressable, so the world's things can be named where a map address does not exist.
- Acceptance: A test reads an answer's distances and finds a unit on each. A test turns a body by degrees in the body frame and in the person's view frame and finds two different headings. A test names a target beside an address with a distance and finds the body at that distance from its surface.
- Relates to: WORLD-004, WORLD-009, WORLD-010, VIEW-005.
- Status: confirmed for address-and-relation first with units and frames on every number, 2026-10-14.

### WORLD-012 A seeded tick record lets any moment be replayed with the input the model had
- Requirement: The host runs on a fixed timestep from a recorded seed, applies operations at recorded tick boundaries, and records for each tick its number, the seed, the operations applied and the observation each agent was given. Any moment can be replayed from that record, with the exact input the model received for the decision it made there.
- What this means: The record is written whether or not anyone is replaying, because its purpose is the moment nobody expected to examine. A tick's observation is the structure of WORLD-009 and WORLD-010 as it was sent, not a reconstruction from the fold. Replay is distinct from re-simulation: replay shows what was received, re-simulation runs the world again from the seed and must report any divergence rather than hide it.
- Why: Designed in, this is a sequence number, a seed and the answer that was already assembled. Added afterwards, it needs the observation to be reconstructable from history, which it is not, because perception depends on what was drawn and loaded at the time. Everything the person's review needs — stepping the frames an agent actually got, linking a decision to its input, forking a run — is a read of this record.
- How it shows: `metaform world ticks` gives the ticks of a run with their seeds and the operations each applied. A decision in a session's transcript links to the tick whose observation it answered.
- Build: The tick number is the host's own counter, and operations carry the tick they were applied at. The record goes beside the log, in the project's store, and is pruned by age with the pruning said plainly rather than silently.
- Acceptance: A test runs the test host twice from one seed with the same operations at the same ticks and finds the same world. A test reads a tick's recorded observation and finds the answer that was sent at that tick, field for field.
- Relates to: WORLD-008, WORLD-009, WORLD-010.
- Status: confirmed for the seeded tick record in cut 1, 2026-10-14. How long ticks are kept is open; a default of one run is recommended.

### WORLD-013 What cut 1 does not include
- Requirement: Cut 1 is WORLD-001 to WORLD-012. The following are named here so they are designed against, and are specified when they are built, not before: ghost previews of the next act in speculative physics with a veto window; the visible lease on a hold with its holder shown and a stop anyone can press; the body's named state and its drawn view cone and gaze, and an edge arrow for a body off-screen; protecting the person's camera, so it moves only when they ask and no body stands in their line of sight; keeping the person's overlays off the layer an agent's camera renders; delivery of events on an agent's next tool result with a wait call so agents do not poll; and the matched trials that test whether a body earns its place.
- What this means: Each of these is a cut 2 item on MIN-13 and each has a seam in cut 1 that it lands behind: the previews behind the host's step call, the lease behind the hold verbs, the body state behind the figure's drawn state, the camera rules behind the person's view, the layers behind the look camera, the events behind the host's subscribe call. None of them is built in cut 1, and none of them requires a change to WORLD-001 to WORLD-012 to be built after it.
- Why: Naming them keeps cut 1 from closing a door on them, and keeps cut 1 from growing to include them.
- Out of scope entirely: the physical-safety regulation the first roundtable drifted into late — contact-force caps, keep-out distances from measured stopping times, pinch gaps, enabling devices, lockout and tagout, crash ratings. The playground is virtual: nothing in it can injure anyone, and none of that is a requirement here. That run's own failure audit flagged the drift. Where a principle from that part of the discussion does apply, it applies as a plain engineering rule already recorded above — limits enforced below the model, WORLD-005; a stop the person can always reach, cut 2; and the simulation stepped by the host and never by a model's turn, WORLD-008.
- Relates to: WORLD-008, MIN-13.
- Status: confirmed as the cut line, 2026-10-14.
