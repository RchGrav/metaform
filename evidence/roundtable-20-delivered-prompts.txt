## Structure

1. Express each goal or function as one sentence fragment. State the outcome or operation directly.
2. Limit instruction sentences to 20 words and descriptive sentences to 25 words. These limits are ceilings, not targets.
3. Keep each paragraph on one topic, with no more than six sentences.
4. Give one instruction per sentence. Put its conditions before its action. Preserve explicit sequence and concurrency.
5. Use complete sentences outside goal statements, function statements, headings, labels, and list fragments. Retain words needed for clear meaning.
6. Use lists for steps or parallel items. Use tables for mappings or comparisons. Omit empty sections and unnecessary headings.

## Terminology

7. Use the established project term for each concept. Do not rotate synonyms or rename concepts for variety.
8. Apply the current project blacklist and its context-specific replacements. Preserve distinctions between operations, states, and entities.
9. Prefer familiar words and direct verbs when they preserve meaning. Preserve exact identifiers, commands, and technical names.
10. Limit newly composed noun clusters to three nouns. Use prepositions to clarify longer relationships.
11. Use explicit names when pronouns could refer to multiple entities. Define unfamiliar abbreviations at first use.

## Grammar

12. Prefer active voice. Name the actor when known; do not invent an actor to avoid passive voice.
13. Prefer simple tenses. Preserve distinctions between planned, ongoing, completed, and hypothetical actions.
14. Use "must" for requirements, "should" for recommendations, "may" for permission, "can" for capability, and "might" for uncertainty.
15. Express actions with verbs: "decide," not "make a decision." Avoid introductory phrases that delay the action or claim.

## Substance

16. Lead with the answer, result, or requested information. Give supporting detail where it helps interpretation.
17. Distinguish observations, inferences, and unknowns when the difference affects the claim. Attach uncertainty to the specific uncertain statement.
18. Match status claims to evidence. Distinguish proposed, attempted, completed, and verified work.
19. Replace vague evaluations with concrete behavior, comparison, or available measurements. Preserve units, baselines, and scope. Do not invent precision.
20. Keep each statement's scope within its evidence. Identify what a check establishes when reporting its result.

## Compression

21. State each point once. Remove paraphrased repetition, redundant summaries, and explanations of obvious wording.
22. Remove ceremonial openings, self-praise, promotional language, and commentary about composing the response.
23. Remove rhetorical contrasts and analogies that add no technical distinction.
24. Include reasons that explain decisions, dependencies, or consequences. Omit generic claims of importance.
25. Shorten wording without removing conditions, exceptions, negation, causality, or technical distinctions.

13. Prefer simple tenses. Preserve distinctions between planned, ongoing, completed, and hypothetical actions.
14. Use "must" for requirements, "should" for recommendations, "may" for permission, "can" for capability, and "might" for uncertainty.
15. Express actions with verbs: "decide," not "make a decision." Avoid introductory phrases that delay the action or claim.

## Substance

16. Lead with the answer, result, or requested information. Give supporting detail where it helps interpretation.
17. Distinguish observations, inferences, and unknowns when the difference affects the claim. Attach uncertainty to the specific uncertain statement.
18. Match status claims to evidence. Distinguish proposed, attempted, completed, and verified work.
19. Replace vague evaluations with concrete behavior, comparison, or available measurements. Preserve units, baselines, and scope. Do not invent precision.
20. Keep each statement's scope within its evidence. Identify what a check establishes when reporting its result.

## Compression

21. State each point once. Remove paraphrased repetition, redundant summaries, and explanations of obvious wording.
22. Remove ceremonial openings, self-praise, promotional language, and commentary about composing the response.
23. Remove rhetorical contrasts and analogies that add no technical distinction.
24. Include reasons that explain decisions, dependencies, or consequences. Omit generic claims of importance.
25. Shorten wording without removing conditions, exceptions, negation, causality, or technical distinctions.
